# B28 - Cyber Safety Flyer Script Content

## Flyer Target Audience

High school students.

## Flyer Topic

Cyber safety for everyday online life.

## Main Message

Think before you click, protect your accounts, and ask for help when something feels suspicious.

---

## Flyer Script Content

### Title

```text
Stay Cyber Safe Online
```

### Subtitle

```text
Simple habits to protect your accounts, privacy, and money.
```

---

## Section 1 - Think Before You Click

```text
Scam messages often try to make you panic or act quickly.

Before clicking a link, stop and check:
- Do I know the sender?
- Does the message sound urgent or threatening?
- Is the link strange or shortened?
- Is it asking for my password, money, or a verification code?

If something feels suspicious, do not click it.
```

### Short Flyer Line

```text
Stop. Check. Verify.
```

---

## Section 2 - Protect Your Passwords

```text
Use strong and unique passwords for important accounts.

Avoid using the same password for:
- school accounts;
- email;
- banking;
- gaming;
- social media.

If one reused password is leaked, attackers may try it on your other accounts.
```

### Short Flyer Line

```text
One account, one password.
```

---

## Section 3 - Turn On Multi-Factor Authentication

```text
Multi-factor authentication, also called MFA, adds an extra layer of protection.

Even if someone gets your password, MFA can make it harder for them to log in.

Use MFA for:
- email;
- school accounts;
- social media;
- banking;
- cloud storage.
```

### Short Flyer Line

```text
MFA helps protect you even if your password is stolen.
```

---

## Section 4 - Never Share Verification Codes

```text
A verification code is like a temporary key to your account.

Do not share codes with anyone, even if they say they are from:
- your bank;
- school IT;
- a delivery company;
- social media support;
- a friend who needs help.

Real support teams should not ask for your login codes.
```

### Short Flyer Line

```text
Your code is for you only.
```

---

## Section 5 - Watch Out for Fake Giveaways

```text
If an offer sounds too good to be true, it might be a scam.

Be careful with messages that promise:
- free money;
- free game skins;
- gift cards;
- prizes;
- follower boosts;
- investment tricks.

Scammers use exciting offers to make people act without thinking.
```

### Short Flyer Line

```text
Too good to be true? Check first.
```

---

## Section 6 - Protect Your Privacy

```text
Think carefully before posting personal information online.

Avoid sharing:
- your home address;
- school timetable;
- private photos;
- phone number;
- passwords;
- location in real time;
- personal documents.

Once something is online, it can be copied, saved, or shared by others.
```

### Short Flyer Line

```text
Post carefully. The internet remembers.
```

---

## Section 7 - What To Do If Something Goes Wrong

```text
If you clicked a suspicious link or shared information, act quickly.

Steps to take:
1. Stop typing information into the page.
2. Change your password.
3. Turn on MFA.
4. Tell a parent, teacher, or school IT support.
5. Report the scam or suspicious account.
6. Check your account activity.
```

### Short Flyer Line

```text
Ask for help early. It is better than hiding the problem.
```

---

## Final Call to Action

```text
Cyber safety is not about being perfect.
It is about slowing down, checking carefully, and asking for help when something seems wrong.
```

### Final Short Message

```text
Pause before you click.
Protect your accounts.
Speak up if something feels wrong.
```

## Reflection

This flyer content was designed for high school students, so the wording is simple and practical. The focus is on everyday online risks such as phishing, password reuse, fake giveaways, verification-code scams, and oversharing personal information.

The strongest message is **Stop. Check. Verify.** This is easy to remember and can help students make safer decisions before clicking links or responding to suspicious messages.

The content also encourages students to ask for help early if something goes wrong. This is important because hiding a mistake can make the harm worse.
