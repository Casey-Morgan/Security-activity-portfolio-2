# B15 - Teaching an Elderly Person About Cybersecurity

## Topic

Recognising scam text messages and avoiding suspicious links.

## Person Taught

My dad.

## Communication Method

iMessage conversation.

## Why I Chose This Topic

I chose this topic because scam messages are common and easy to receive on a phone. The advice is simple, practical, and useful for everyday life. It is especially important because scam messages may pretend to be from banks, delivery companies, government services, or family members.

## Key Points Taught

- Do not tap links in unexpected text messages.
- Open official apps or websites directly instead of using message links.
- Watch for urgent language, spelling mistakes, fake numbers, and unrealistic offers.
- Never share passwords or verification codes by SMS.
- Ask a trusted person before responding to a suspicious message.

---

## Reflection

This activity helped me practise explaining cybersecurity in simple language. Instead of using technical terms, I focused on clear everyday examples such as bank alerts and parcel delivery messages.

The most useful advice was the rule: **stop, check, and ask before clicking**. This is easy to remember and can help prevent phishing, account theft, and financial scams.
