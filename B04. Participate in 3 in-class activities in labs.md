# B04 - Participate in 3 In-Class Activities in Labs

## Overview

This portfolio entry documents participation in three in-class lab activities from CITS2006. The activities covered privacy, access control, and vulnerability analysis. These topics are important foundations in defensive cybersecurity because they help explain how systems protect data, control permissions, and identify weaknesses before attackers can exploit them.

---

## Lab 2 - Privacy

### Summary

This lab focused on privacy concepts and how personal information can be exposed, collected, linked, or misused. The activity helped me understand that privacy is not only about hiding information, but also about controlling how information is collected, processed, stored, and shared.

### Key Concepts Practised

- personal information and sensitive information;
- privacy risks in digital systems;
- data collection and data minimisation;
- privacy leakage through online activity;
- the importance of informed consent;
- privacy protection as part of cybersecurity design.

### What I Learned

I learned that privacy risks can occur even when a system is not directly hacked. For example, users may reveal information through normal online activity, poor privacy settings, or unnecessary data collection by applications.

A key lesson from this lab was that secure systems should collect only the data they need and should protect that data throughout its lifecycle.

### Defensive Cybersecurity Relevance

Privacy is closely connected to cybersecurity because leaked personal information can be used for identity theft, phishing, social engineering, account recovery attacks, and targeted scams.

## Lab 3 - Access Control

### Summary

This lab focused on access control, which is the process of deciding who is allowed to access specific resources and what actions they are allowed to perform. Access control is one of the most important parts of system security because many security incidents happen when users have too much access or when permissions are incorrectly configured.

### Key Concepts Practised

- authentication and authorisation;
- users, roles, and permissions;
- least privilege;
- role-based access control;
- access control policies;
- risks caused by weak or incorrect permission settings.

### What I Learned

I learned that authentication and authorisation are different. Authentication checks identity, while authorisation checks what the user is allowed to do after their identity is known.

A key lesson from this lab was that users should only receive the minimum permissions required for their role. This reduces damage if an account is compromised or misused.

### Defensive Cybersecurity Relevance

Strong access control helps prevent unauthorised access to sensitive files, administrator functions, user accounts, and system settings. It also supports accountability because systems can track which users performed which actions.

## Lab 4 - Vulnerability Analysis


### Summary

This lab focused on vulnerability analysis. The activity helped me understand how security weaknesses can be identified, assessed, and prioritised. Vulnerability analysis is important because organisations need to find and fix weaknesses before attackers can take advantage of them.

### Key Concepts Practised

- identifying security weaknesses;
- understanding vulnerability impact;
- assessing likelihood and risk;
- prioritising remediation;
- interpreting vulnerability information;
- communicating findings clearly.

### What I Learned

I learned that not all vulnerabilities have the same level of risk. Some vulnerabilities may be easy to exploit and have serious impact, while others may be lower priority because they are difficult to exploit or affect less important systems.

A key lesson from this lab was that vulnerability analysis should include both technical detail and risk context. This helps organisations decide what to fix first.

### Defensive Cybersecurity Relevance

Vulnerability analysis supports proactive defence. By identifying weak points early, defenders can patch systems, improve configuration, reduce exposure, and prevent avoidable incidents.

## Overall Reflection

Participating in these three labs helped me connect cybersecurity theory with practical activities. The privacy lab showed why protecting personal information matters. The access control lab showed how permissions can protect or expose systems. The vulnerability analysis lab showed how defenders identify and prioritise weaknesses.

Together, these activities demonstrated that cybersecurity is not just one technical control. It involves protecting data, managing access, and continuously identifying risks.

The most important lesson I learned was that defensive cybersecurity should be proactive. Systems should be designed to reduce privacy risk, enforce least privilege, and identify vulnerabilities before they become serious incidents.
